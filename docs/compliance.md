# What the record checks mean

Sources opened 10 November 2026. These checks support an estimator's review. They are not a legal compliance certificate, and no threshold below is presented as a statutory safe harbour. This base does not accept contracts, process payments or determine tax.

| Rule | Check | Basis and limit |
|---|---|---|
| NZ-PRICE | No estimate lines, missing hourly rates, missing acceptance criteria or missing estimate evidence | [Commerce Commission: quotes, estimates and invoicing](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/business/dealing-with-typical-situations/quotes-estimates-and-invoicing/). Clear scope and price records support reliable estimates. These specific required fields are our implementation policy, not fields prescribed by that page. |
| NZ-IPP9 | Contact held without a recorded purpose or with no current retention review date | [Privacy Commissioner: Principle 9](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/9/). The principle limits retention beyond lawful need. A review date is a prompt to assess that need, not a required retention period or an automatic deletion instruction. |
| INTERNAL-SCOPE | Open assumptions before submission | [ScopeStack approval workflow](https://docs.scopestack.io/help/projects/approval-workflow). This is a business control inspired by revision-based review, not a law. |

A technical reviewer checks the scope and effort. A separate commercial reviewer checks the price and any margin exception. Neither can be the recorded scope owner. Their operator labels and evidence are retained. These labels do not establish identity: production deployment needs authentication and reviewed privileges.

The demo's 30 percent margin threshold and 90-day rate-review interval are editable internal policies. No age test proves a rate is accurate. Review assumptions with the actual client. Statements of work explicitly say draft estimate, show currency and a required tax note, and record no customer acceptance.

These sources concern New Zealand. Before using the base for an Australian business, review the applicable rules and adapt the checks. Protect personal details in the database, activity history, snapshots and generated reports. Do not erase approval evidence or silently delete records to clear a finding. A shared deployment needs access controls, a retention plan and tested private backups.
